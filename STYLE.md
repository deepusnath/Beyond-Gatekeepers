# Voice & Style Guide

How the Purple Movement writes, in this repo and everywhere. Contributors: match this voice in charters, playbooks, and session logs.

## Voice

- Clear, direct, statesmanlike. Bold but responsible. Critical but not cynical.
- Builder-to-builder: name the person, the date, the venue, the lesson. No filler.
- Visionary and implementable in the same sentence. If it can't be acted on, cut it.

## Hard rules

1. **No em dashes.** Use a comma, a colon, a period, or parentheses instead.
2. **No unsupported claims.** Counts, dates, titles, and partnerships get verified before they're written. If unverified, label it.
3. **Label the status of ideas.** Confirmed fact, founder intention, working proposal, or open question. Never present a proposal as a settled decision.
4. **Credit is collective.** Community history names contributors, volunteers, leads, and partner institutions. No single-hero narratives, including about the founder.
5. **Systems, not people, get criticized.** We critique broken structures; we do not attack individuals.
6. **No corporate filler, no clichés, no AI-sounding prose.** If a sentence would fit in any company's press release, rewrite it.

## Preferences

- Short paragraphs. Tables for facts. Prose for reasoning.
- Frameworks over adjectives: ladders, flywheels, checklists, operating models.
- English as the working language; session logs may add a local-language summary.
- Honest limitations stated in the same document as the ambition.

## Why this exists

The movement's credibility is its only asset. Every unverified number, inflated claim, or hero narrative spends credibility the communities earned in the room.
